# Post 16 — style guide

Quiet, reverent, intimate short film about unseen caregiving, framed by Bhagavad Gita 18.9. Two visual modes share one palette and grade. (1) Modern scenes: photorealistic, cinematic, 35-50mm lens, shallow depth of field, natural motivated light, handheld-steady slow push-ins. Faces of modern people are never shown or identifiable; use hands, backs, silhouettes and soft focus. (2) Sacred and symbolic scenes: painterly, in the manner of traditional Indian devotional painting with soft oil and gouache texture, reverent and dignified, with traditional iconography. Shared palette: pre-dawn indigo and soft blue moving toward warm marigold, saffron gold, brass and diya-flame amber; muted earthy neutrals; gentle film grain; low contrast with lifted blacks. Mood: tender, unhurried, dignified, never sentimental or melodramatic. Recurring motifs: the weekly pill organizer, a cup of chai or tea, a brass diya, a marigold, a flowing river at sunrise. No text, letters, numbers, logos or watermarks are generated in any visual; all on-screen text and branding are added in editing. Leave calm negative space in the upper or lower third for captions. The master is 4:5; keep key subjects centered so a 9:16 reframe works.

## Scene 0 · voiceover · 4:5

Warm, calm, gentle narrator voice, mature and unhurried, with a soft Indian-English accent. Tone is compassionate and reflective, never preachy. Read slightly slower and with reverence on the verse, with a brief pause before and after it, and pronounce 'Arjuna' and 'sattvic tyaga' correctly. Script: "No one applauds the 6am pill reminder. The Gita has a word for this kind of work. [pause] Hospital forms. Clinic corridors. The weekend drive home that nobody notices. [pause] He who performeth a prescribed action, saying, 'it ought to be done,' O Arjuna, relinquishing attachment and also fruit, that relinquishment is regarded as pure. Bhagavad Gita, chapter 18, verse 9. [pause] Krishna calls this sattvic tyaga, pure relinquishment. Traditional commentators broadly agree: it does not mean giving up the action, but letting go of clinging to it and to its reward. [pause] A reflection, not scripture: perhaps some of the weight we carry comes less from the work itself, and more from waiting to be thanked. [pause] Wanting to be seen is human. Still, the care can stand on its own, done simply because it ought to be done. [pause] What are you doing today simply because it ought to be done?"
