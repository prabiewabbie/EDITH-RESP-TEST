# Post 1 — style guide

Two visual registers tied by one warm dawn palette. (A) Modern room, scenes 1-3 and 6-7: photorealistic and cinematic. A quiet, minimal room with pale wood floor and off-white walls. One window casts soft golden morning light; dust motes float in the air. Shot on a 35mm or 50mm lens, shallow depth of field, slow motivated camera moves, gentle film grain. The meditator's age and identity stay indeterminate, seen from behind or the side or in hand close-ups; the face is never clearly visible. Simple neutral clothing in oatmeal and soft grey. (B) Gita scenes 4-5: reverent painterly classical Indian style inspired by traditional miniature and devotional painting. Soft gouache texture, fine linework and a luminous dawn sky. Krishna appears in traditional iconography: blue-hued skin, peacock feather crown, yellow silk pitambara, serene expression. Arjuna appears as a dignified warrior-prince in attentive, respectful posture. White horses, an ornate wooden chariot. Armies appear only as distant soft silhouettes. Shared palette: honey gold, saffron, rose-gold, warm cream, sage green, with deep blue accents in the Gita scenes. Mood: calm, compassionate, unhurried. Transitions are soft cross-dissolves and light blooms. No text in any generated frame; all on-screen text and the channel title are added in editing. Master aspect 4:5, with protected center-safe framing so assets can be reframed to 9:16.

[pause] Then comes the familiar thought: I'm just bad at this. [pause] In the Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 6, verse 26, Krishna says: As often as the wavering and unsteady mind goeth forth, so often reining it in, let him bring it under the control of the Self. [pause] Notice what the verse assumes. The mind will go forth. The teaching is to bring it back, as often as it wanders. [pause] A reflection, not scripture: perhaps each gentle return is the practice itself. Not proof that you failed, but the very thing you sat down to do. [pause] So when your mind drifts, try meeting it with patience instead of harsh judgement. Then simply come back. [pause] Tomorrow morning, five minutes again. Count the returns, not the wanderings.'
