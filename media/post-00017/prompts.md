# Post 17 — style guide

Contemplative, reverent cinematic short in 4:5 master framing (center-weighted so it reframes cleanly to 9:16). Two visual worlds share one palette. (1) The modern monsoon city is photorealistic, shot on a 35mm or 50mm prime with shallow depth of field and gentle handheld drift. It begins with muted warm dust-ochre and slate-grey clouds, then shifts to cool silver rain light and finally warm amber streetlight and golden break-through sky. (2) The scripture scene and the essence montage are painterly, inspired by Indian miniature painting with cinematic volumetric light. They use soft gold, saffron, peacock blue, terracotta red earth and deep green. Light is always soft and diffused, never harsh. Grading is gentle filmic with low contrast, lifted shadows, fine grain and no oversaturation. The mood moves from hurried, to pause, to wonder, to quiet remembrance. Every human figure is anonymous and does not resemble any real person. Divine figures are always shown with dignity in traditional iconography. No text, letters, logos, watermarks or signage are rendered in any frame; all on-screen text and branding are added in editing.

## Scene 0 · voiceover · 4:5

Warm, calm, gender-neutral or soft adult voice with a gentle Indian-English accent. The tone is intimate and reflective, like a friend speaking softly and never preachy. The pace is unhurried, with natural pauses between lines and a reverent hush on the verse. Script: "You know that smell when the first rain hits dry ground, and for one second you forget your inbox? ... The first drops fall. And from the earth itself, a fragrance rises. ... Just for a moment, you stop. You breathe. ... In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna says: 'The pure fragrance of earths and the brilliance in fire am I; the life in all beings am I, and the austerity in ascetics.' Bhagavad Gita, chapter 7, verse 9. ... Traditional commentators broadly read this passage as Krishna pointing to the Divine as the essence that makes each thing what it is: fragrance in earth, brilliance in fire, life in every being. ... Reflection: perhaps the sacred isn't found only in temples. It may already be present in what we sense every ordinary day. And a single pause of wonder can become a moment of remembrance. ... Next time the rain comes, let it remind you."

Negative: robotic tone, dramatic movie-trailer voice, shouting, comedic delivery, celebrity voice imitation, background noise, mispronunciation of Bhagavad Gita or Krishna
